# Expanded panorama prompts

Generated with the built-in image generation tool. The early sheet used `historical-scenes.png` as its style reference. The other two sheets used `early-panorama.png`. Original generated outputs were copied into this directory without modifying their pixels.

## early-panorama.png

Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: production atlas of SEVEN horizontal Bayeux embroidery panoramas for Paper Trails.
The provided image is ONLY a STYLE REFERENCE. Make NEW expanded narrative artwork, not a copy of the grid.
Output one landscape canvas about 2800 by 1750 pixels containing EXACTLY SEVEN equally high horizontal strips stacked top to bottom. Each strip runs the entire canvas width, with NO columns, NO boxes, NO gutters, NO writing, NO labels, NO border frames. Each full-width strip is ONE continuous panorama devoted to ONE historical subject described below. All strips share the exact same warm pale unbleached linen base, light flat stitched colors, madder red, indigo, sage and ochre. Flat expressive profile people with stitched outlines and small wool stitches, Bayeux Tapestry aesthetic, historically appropriate clothing and objects for their respective eras. Thin curling stitched ground and plants join the action groups so there is no empty interval. Keep a consistent ground baseline and fill the middle 85% of each strip with action. Leave the top/bottom 4% of each strip as identical plain pale linen, without dividing lines. Render ample fine details without dark photographic shading.
Within EACH strip, show five distinct narrative groups flowing LEFT TO RIGHT, each occupying roughly one fifth of the width; do not repeat a figure group. Surround and connect them with textiles, plants, landscape and architecture. No hard scene boundaries. The first group is the clear iconic summary; following groups add complementary details, not the same picture enlarged. These are symbolic facets of existing database events, not extra dated incidents. No gore or generic medieval clothing replacing later period uniforms.
The seven strips from TOP to BOTTOM are:
1 Renaissance: scholars with an open manuscript; humanist discussion around a table; artist painting a portrait; sculptor carving classical forms; readers sharing printed books beside classical architecture.
2 Fall of Constantinople: Ottoman cannon and city walls; gunners moving powder and cannonballs; defenders on walls with Byzantine towers; ships on the Bosphorus; merchants and the captured city's domes.
3 Early Columbian Exchange: Atlantic sailing ship between shores; maize potatoes and diverse crops carried in baskets; horses and livestock crossing a landing place; encounters between people from Europe Africa and the Americas with historically varied dress, NO stereotypes; caregivers attending sick people, soberly acknowledging harmful consequences.
4 Protestant Reformation: reformer beside a printing press; typesetter arranging movable type; printers hanging pamphlets; readers discussing texts in a street; divided religious communities beside churches.
5 Little Ice Age: village under falling snow; figures crossing a frozen river; skaters and sledges on ice; farmers tending cold fields; people gathering firewood near homes.
6 Thirty Years War: seventeenth-century pikemen; musketeers in a field; artillery and carts; damaged village and displaced civilians; a weary camp with cooking fires.
7 English Civil War: opposing Royalist and Parliamentarian soldiers; cavalry with period dress; pikemen and musketeers; fortified town with cannon; civilians and a political meeting.
Keep seven strips in this exact order, with five different action groups PER strip. Every strip is about 11:1 panorama within the overall 1.6:1 canvas.
