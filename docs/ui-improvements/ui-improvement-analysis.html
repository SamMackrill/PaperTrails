Decisions that shaped this review

Your answers from the five question rounds set the priorities throughout the document.

Goals & audience
  • Audience: researchers and enthusiasts, so information density and precision count.
  • Devices: desktop-first; mobile has to stay usable.
  • Core job: show the big picture to a first-time visitor.
  • Scope: fixes plus moderate enhancements that fit the no-build architecture.
Timeline
  • Offer an optional density scale; linear stays the default.
  • Replace number bubbles with face stacks.
  • New YAML fields are fine (for example, notability).
  • Stack publications into a mini-histogram.
Navigation
  • Search everything, grouped by type.
  • Deep links are a nice-to-have.
  • Evaluate a minimap, a shortcuts dialog, and back/forward in details.
  • Show relationships on hover or selection only.
Look & feel
  • A non-modal docked panel on desktop.
  • Lean into a paper / archival identity.
  • Treat Tapestry as a showpiece.
  • Keep the greyscale rest state for portraits.

What already works well

  • The data model is clean and semantic. Scientists, publications, discoveries (with scientist_ids and theorist_ids), conferences (with attendees and theorists) and events already hold the links a research-grade UI needs. Most of the recommendations below are presentation work, not data work.
  • Accessible names are thorough. Every marker is a real <button> with a descriptive aria-label, the panel returns focus on close, and prefers-reduced-motion is respected.
  • The theme loads without a flash. The inline script in index.html sets the theme before first paint, and color-scheme: only stops browsers auto-darkening the page.
  • Event labels follow the viewport. updateEventLabelPositions keeps long-band titles in view while panning and falls back to shortTitle. Few timelines do this.
  • The conference panel is the strongest screen. It has an attendee portrait grid, separate theorists, map and period-map links, and a credited archival photo. It's a good template for the other panel types.
  • The Tapestry artwork is distinctive and gives the project a memorable identity. It deserves more prominence (see Tapestry).
  • It works offline with no build step, including a vendored YAML parser. Every recommendation here keeps to that constraint.

Layout & responsive bugs

L1

The toolbar overflows at tablet and small-laptop widths, and filter buttons cover Tapestry and Illustrations

Problem

Between the 768px mobile breakpoint and roughly 1,100–1,200px, the secondary toolbar's contents are wider than the space left over. .toolbar-secondary has min-width: 0 and its buttons use white-space: nowrap, so everything overlaps instead of wrapping. The 980px breakpoint only reduces padding. I confirmed this at 1024px and 844px; 1280px and above are fine.

Why it matters
iPads, small laptops and split-screen browser windows all land in this range. Two controls become unreadable and partly unclickable.
Recommendation

Pick one:

  • Move the Options collapse breakpoint up to about 1180px, or use a container query on .timeline-toolbar so it collapses based on the space it actually has.
  • Or split into two rows: layers on one row, display modes (Tapestry, Illustrations) on the other.
  • Or make Tapestry and Illustrations icon-only below about 1280px, with the label in a tooltip.

Add a regression screenshot at 1024px to the verification checklist in ui-refresh-plan.md.

L2

Short historical events draw a vertical line through their own labels

Problem
When an event lasts only a year or two, the band is a few pixels wide and the label is centred over it. The band's border and fill then run straight through the text, so it reads as struck through.
Why it matters
It looks like a rendering bug, and it affects about a third of the context events at the default zoom.
Recommendation
If a band is narrower than about 24px, draw it as a pin: a short tick or dot on the band row, with the label beside it in a chip that has a surface background and sits above the band. Wider bands keep the current behaviour. Put the label's background on .event-title so it covers any band lines underneath.

L3

Context labels pile on top of each other when they run out of rows

Problem
Row assignment estimates label width as title.length × 6.5 + 12. When no row is free, it falls back to the last row regardless of collisions.
Why it matters
The context lane is how the timeline shows the big picture, and on narrower screens it becomes unreadable exactly where events are densest.
Recommendation
Measure labels properly with a shared canvas measureText, which is cheap and needs no DOM. Degrade in steps: full title, then shortTitle, then a pin with no label (still focusable, with a tooltip). Never place an item where it overlaps another. If rows are still exhausted, let the context lane grow into spare space (see L7).

Timeline readability & density

The main goal is the big picture: showing how science accelerated and in what historical setting. At the default zoom the timeline currently looks like a scatter of numbers and diamonds. Most of the items in this section aim to make the overview readable at a glance.

T1

Replace anonymous number bubbles with face stacks

Problem
At the overview zoom (scale below 1.5), people are grouped into fixed 14-year buckets however wide the screen is, and any group larger than one becomes a numbered circle. The overview hides the scientists, which are the product's main content.
Recommendation
  • Draw each cluster as a face stack: two or three overlapping greyscale portraits and a "+N" badge. Choose the faces by notability (T5), falling back to the most publications.
  • Keep the current tooltip listing all names, and keep click-to-zoom.
  • Group by pixel distance rather than a fixed year bucket, so wide screens split clusters and phones merge them (this also fixes M1).
  • At standard zoom, keep single portraits and show a name label for top-notability people (T6).
Code
src/timelineRenderer.js:151–255 (renderScientists) · bucket size at :165
T2

Stack publications into a mini-histogram along the axis

Problem
Only publications with the same year are nudged apart (7px each). Neighbouring years overlap, so all 237 markers sit on one line and the densest decades become an unreadable strip.
Recommendation
Bin publications by pixel column (one bin per marker width at the current zoom) and stack them upward from the axis. The stack height then works as a histogram of scientific output, a big-picture signal you get for free. Colour each marker with the scientist's colour on hover (as now). Cap stacks at about 6 with a "+N" cap marker that zooms in when clicked. The scientist connector lines should end at the specific stacked marker.

T3

Add an optional "even density" time scale

Problem
With a linear scale, 1400–1650 takes up 40% of the width but holds a handful of items, while 1850–1950 (about 60% of the content) gets 16%.
Recommendation
  • Add a toggle, Even time / Even density, next to Fit. Linear stays the default.
  • Density mode uses a piecewise-linear mapping: split the range into eras (for example by century, subdividing after 1800), weight each era by item count with a floor so quiet eras don't disappear, and normalise.
  • Make the distortion visible. Shade alternate segments and label them ("1 cm ≈ 40 years" / "≈ 5 years"), or put axis break marks at segment joins. Researchers must never read positions as linear by mistake.
  • Do the plumbing first. All positioning should go through one yearToX/xToYear pair. At the moment script.js focusYear and tapestryRenderer.js layoutTapestry each re-implement the linear maths.

Navigation & wayfinding

N1

Add global search across every content type

Problem
There's no way to find Maxwell except by panning, zooming and hovering. For a researcher audience this is the biggest functional gap.
Recommendation
  • Put a search field in the header (the space is currently empty), opened with / or Ctrl+K.
  • Index scientists, publications, discoveries, conferences and events from the already-loaded YAML. That's about 430 records, so a plain in-memory filter is enough.
  • Make matching diacritic-insensitive using normalize('NFD') and stripping combining marks, then transliterate letters that NFD leaves alone (ø→o, æ→ae, œ→oe, ß→ss, ł→l, đ→d, þ→th). Apply the same folding to indexed names and to queries. The data is full of names like Ørsted, Schrödinger, Rømer, Ampère and Poincaré, and searches for "Orsted" and "Romer" must find them.
  • Group results by type, with year and a small portrait or shape. Rank by exact prefix, then notability (T5).
  • Choosing a result animates to its year (N3), selects it and opens the docked panel (D1).
  • The combobox needs full ARIA (role="combobox", aria-activedescendant, arrow keys, Esc).
N2

Add an overview minimap

Problem
Once you zoom in, nothing shows where you are in the 626-year range or where the dense areas are. The zoom percentage doesn't tell you what's on screen.
Recommendation
Add a thin strip (about 28px) between the toolbar and the frame, showing the whole range as a density sparkline of all items, coloured by type, with century ticks. Overlay a draggable viewport rectangle: drag it to pan, drag its edges or brush to zoom, and click elsewhere to jump. It gives first-time visitors the big picture at a glance and keeps them oriented while zoomed. It depends on the shared scale functions (T3).
N3

Animate zoom and jump transitions

Problem
Clicking a cluster, "Locate on timeline", Fit, or the zoom buttons changes the view instantly, so users lose track of where they came from.
Recommendation
Tween currentScale and currentTranslateX over about 250–300ms with an ease-out curve. During the tween, apply a CSS transform: scale() to the existing DOM, and re-lay out once at the end (see R2). Skip the tween when prefers-reduced-motion is set.

N4

Make zoom logarithmic and show the visible range

Problem
The slider maps linearly from 1× to 8×, so most of its travel is spent at high magnification, and at rest its thumb sits at the far left next to "100%". A percentage doesn't tell a researcher which years are on screen.
Recommendation
Map the slider logarithmically (scale = MIN × (MAX/MIN)^t). Replace or supplement the percentage with the visible range ("1848–1931"), which can also be the minimap's label. Consider raising MAX_SCALE: at 8× the 1920s are still crowded.

N7

Trackpad pinch zooms far too fast

Problem
Every Ctrl-wheel event applies a fixed ±20% step, whatever its deltaY. Trackpad pinches fire dozens of small events, so one gesture goes from 1× to 8× almost instantly.
Recommendation
Scale by magnitude: factor = Math.exp(-deltaY × 0.0015), normalised for deltaMode (lines vs pixels). Mouse wheels still step comfortably and trackpads become smooth.

N8

Put the view and selection in the URL so they can be shared

Recommendation
Encode the view in the hash, for example #from=1850&to=1950&item=scientist:maxwell&layers=-pubs. Use history.replaceState for panning and zooming (debounced) and pushState for selections, so the browser's Back button steps through what you've opened (see N9). Restore the view on load. Researchers can then cite a view in notes or share it with students.

Details panel

D1

Dock the panel beside the timeline instead of making it modal

Problem
The panel dims the timeline, blocks it with a backdrop and traps focus. To compare two scientists you have to close the panel, find the second one and reopen it, and the selected marker is hidden behind the backdrop.
Recommendation
  • On screens 1024px and wider, render the panel as a third grid column of .timeline-frame (about 400px) and re-lay out the timeline in the remaining width. There's no backdrop and no focus trap. Clicking another item swaps the panel's content.
  • Use role="complementary" with aria-labelledby. On open, move focus to the heading; Esc closes the panel and returns focus to the item.
  • Keep the current modal bottom sheet on mobile.
  • This needs C3 (selection surviving re-renders) done first.

Connections & research context

C1

Show a scientist's relationships when they're hovered or selected

Problem
Highlighting currently links a person only to their own publications. The richer data (discoveries confirming a theory, conference attendance) isn't shown on the canvas.
Recommendation
Keep the resting state calm, as agreed. On hover or selection, draw curved connectors from the person to their discoveries and conferences, and faint lines to people they attended a conference with. Dim everything else. The reverse should also work: hovering a conference lights up its attendees' portraits, and hovering a discovery lights up its discoverer and theorists. Reuse the reverse index from D2.

C2

Show a lifespan bar on hover and selection

Recommendation
Portraits sit at the year of the first publication, so a scientist's lifetime is invisible. On hover or selection, draw a thin birth-to-death bar in the People lane behind the portrait, labelled at each end. It answers "who overlapped with whom" at a glance, a question the timeline is well placed to answer. The birth and death dates are already in the data.

Recommendation

Accessibility

A1

Tabbing to an off-screen item scrolls the canvas out of step with panning

Problem
Measured: focusing the last publication marker makes the browser scroll the overflow: hidden container natively (scrollLeft goes from 0 to 1040) while the app's translateX stays the same. From then on, dragging, zoom anchors and label positioning are all offset by 1,040px until the page is reloaded.
Recommendation
In a focusin handler on the container, reset timelineContainer.scrollLeft = 0 and adjust currentTranslateX so the focused element is in view (with padding), then call updateTransform(). Guard against scrolling in a scroll listener as a fallback.

A2

There are over 330 tab stops, in non-chronological order

Problem

R1

Full-size PNGs are served for 42px portraits and the Tapestry

Problem
images/cartoons/ holds 121 PNGs totalling 166 MB (about 1.4 MB each, up to 2 MB), shown at 42px. Turning on Illustrations while zoomed in starts dozens of megabytes of downloads. R2

Every zoom step rebuilds the whole DOM, including on every pinch move

Problem
render() clears the timeline and rebuilds several hundred elements (and re-attaches their listeners) on each zoom click, each wheel tick and every touchmove of a pinch. On phones this makes pinch-zoom stutter, and it's also why hover state and selection are lost (C3).
Recommendation
During continuous gestures, apply transform: scale() to the existing layer and do a full layout once on gesture end, or at most once per animation frame (requestAnimationFrame coalescing). Use event delegation on #timeline instead of per-marker listeners, which cuts the rebuild cost.

Dependencies between items

  • C3 (selection persists) → D1 (docked panel). Without C3, docking will clear the selection on every zoom.
  • Shared yearToX/xToYear → T3 (density scale), N2 (minimap), N4 (visible range). Consolidate the three copies of the scale maths first.
