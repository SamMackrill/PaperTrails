# Karlsruhe, Paris and the periodic table

Added four scientist profiles (`cannizzaro`, `mendeleev`, `meyer`, `avogadro`), eight publications, the 1860 Karlsruhe Congress, the 1900 International Congress of Physics in Paris, and the 1869 Periodic Table of Elements discovery.

## Historical decisions and sources

- Karlsruhe met on 3–5 September 1860 in the Ständehaus. Cannizzaro, Mendeleev and Meyer attended; Avogadro died in 1856 and is linked separately through the existing `theorist_ids` field. Sources: [KIT congress exhibition](https://www.bibliothek.kit.edu/chemiker-kongress.php), [KIT anniversary account](https://www.kit.edu/downloads/pi/PI_2010_094_engl_Milestone_in_the_History_of_Chemistry.pdf), and [Science History Institute on Meyer and Mendeleev](https://www.sciencehistory.org/education/scientific-biographies/julius-lothar-meyer-and-dmitri-ivanovich-mendeleev/).
- The discovery marker is anchored to Mendeleev's 1869 publication, while its text distinguishes Meyer's 1864 partial classification and 1870 expanded table/atomic-volume curve. They worked independently. The Science History Institute account above documents this chronology.
- Paris met on 6–10 August 1900 during the Exposition Universelle. Its organizer, the [Société Française de Physique](https://www.sfphysique.fr/sfp-tradition-ouverture-scientifique/), explicitly identifies the eight linked existing profiles as present: Becquerel, Marie Curie, Poincaré, J. J. Thomson, Kelvin, Lorentz, Birkeland and Eötvös. This is a selected list, not a claim to a complete roster. See also [Guillaume's contemporary report, Nature 62, 425–428](https://doi.org/10.1038/062425b0).
- The Karlsruhe historical map is a retrospective reproduction of a plan from the **late 1850s**, not a precisely dated 1860 map: [BLB catalogue](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/structure/264911), [map page](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/pageview/401790). Its year label preserves that uncertainty.
- The Paris map is the BnF's 1900 exposition plan, GED-3472: [archival scan](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b84422615/f1.item), [North Carolina State University metadata identifying the map and date](https://scalar.chass.ncsu.edu/bodies-and-structures-2/media/exposition-universelle-de-1900-plan-d-ensemble.meta?versions=1). Both archive viewers rejected automated web-tool access with HTTP 403; their catalogue references were verified separately.

Five new affiliation rows deliberately use the neutral icon: Genoa, Palermo and Rome for Cannizzaro; Saint Petersburg for Mendeleev; Turin for Avogadro. README's neutral-affiliation count is now 38.

## Portraits

Source portraits are stored at `images/{cannizzaro,mendeleev,meyer,avogadro}.jpg`; individual illustrations are stored at the corresponding `images/cartoons/<id>.png` paths. Credits, archive links and rights statements are in [images/ATTRIBUTION.md](../images/ATTRIBUTION.md).

The built-in image-generation tool produced four separate illustrations, using each source portrait for identity and Maxwell/Bohr for house style. Raw images had genuine alpha but were 1254×1254; the repository finisher normalized them to 1024×1024 and cleared canvas-corner pixels. It removed zero background pixels. All four finished candidates passed the asset validator before promotion and were inspected alongside their sources, on contrasting backgrounds, and in 92 px colour and 42 px grayscale circles. See the [portrait proof](chemistry-additions-2026-09-17-portraits.png). The shared question mark in the comparison represents the absence of a previous portrait for these new profiles.
