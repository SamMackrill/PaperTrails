# Understanding charge: editorial and source audit

Eight selected stops support the prototype research task: distinguish charge's sign, force law, field role, carrier and measured unit. This is an interpretive route, not a complete genealogy. It omits many contributors and alternatives; expansion should follow sourced relationships, not make the initial release a coverage project.

Each YAML stop and transition contains labelled primary evidence with a locator. Publisher DOI metadata and original-author texts were checked on 29 September 2026. Public-domain transcriptions supplement original scans. No source text is quoted at length.

| Stop | Evidence and dating decision |
|---|---|
| Franklin, 1751 | First-edition title/date also checked against Library of Congress item 04006387 and Smithsonian catalogue. The charged-bottle observations describe positive/negative accounting. The 1752 kite does not belong in this edition's summary. |
| Coulomb, 1785 | Académie des sciences hosts the first memoir, pp. 569–577. Its bibliography explicitly distinguishes academy year 1785 from printing in 1788. Keep the academy year with a prominent qualification. |
| Faraday, 1832 | Publisher DOI 10.1098/rstl.1832.0006 identifies the journal publication as 1832, pp. 125–162. Correct the existing publication year from 1831; preserve the stable ID and old link. Experiments and presentation remain dated 1831 in the qualification. |
| Faraday, 1846 | Publisher DOI 10.1098/rstl.1846.0001 identifies the nineteenth series, pp. 1–20. Smithsonian's *On the magnetization of light* catalogue corroborates the edition. Replace the inaccurate summary with polarization rotation. Experiments/presentation were in 1845. |
| Maxwell, 1873 | The Treatise preface explicitly credits reading Faraday's *Experimental Researches* before the mathematics. Volume II, Part IV, Chapter XX supplies the theory-of-light locator. The influence applies to Faraday's corpus; selecting the nineteenth series does not prove that single paper was the exclusive cause. |
| Hertz, 1888 | Publisher DOI 10.1002/andp.18882700802 gives pp. 609–623. Hertz's collected *Electric Waves*, English translation first published 1893, reprints this as Chapter VIII, pp. 124–134. Use Hertz's own Introduction for theoretical framing, not Kelvin's foreword. The archive OCR was inspected; a later reprint can retain the 1893 text and pagination. |
| Thomson, 1897 | *Cathode Rays*, pp. 293–316, contrasts ether processes with charged matter and measures mass divided by charge. Remove the 1904 plum-pudding model from this paper's summary. The ratio does not independently fix charge or mass. |
| Millikan, 1913 | Publisher DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.2.109 confirms 1 August 1913, pp. 109–143. The AIP-hosted original facsimile describes charge multiples. Add a narrowly scoped discovery record rather than an unsourced new biography. This historical estimate is not the modern exact SI definition. |

Five transitions are explicitly **conceptual bridges**. Their sources establish the compared results, not an otherwise undocumented citation or causal influence. The Faraday-to-Maxwell transition is documented influence with the corpus limitation stated. Maxwell-to-Hertz is a prediction/test relationship. Hertz-to-Thomson compares distinct phenomena and must never imply that cathode rays are electromagnetic waves. Thomson's competing explanations belong in his stop rather than an invented competing-theories edge between these two selected papers.

Original records retain IDs and aliases. Newly appended Millikan discovery freezes `discovery:39` as its alias. No new researcher profile, portrait or broad coverage expansion is introduced.
